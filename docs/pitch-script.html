Voice-over — why this beats a fixed array

Three reasons this beats bolting panels to the ground.

One: there is no installation. The panel arrives already mounted on the thing that carries it. You unload it, and it is working.

Two: nothing is dug and nothing is bolted down. The field is still a field — and a machine that can drive away again is a far smaller thing to get permission for.

Three: you were going to buy the batteries anyway. Midday solar already beats midday demand in market after market, and daytime prices are heading to zero. Any new array needs storage to be worth building.

So if the battery is compulsory — and the battery is the part that leaves the field — why pay for the installation at all? The energy goes out in a box, not a trench.

Voice-over — how it actually works

Each sheep is a four-wheel skid-steer rover. Panel on top, a four-kilo, five-hundred-watt-hour pack slung underneath.

Skid steer means heading is panel orientation — so driving and sun-tracking are one control problem, not two.

And the driving is trained, not scripted: four thousand rovers in parallel on a single GPU, PPO, over a curriculum of rubble, slope and obstacles that gets harder as the policy gets better.

When a pack fills, the sheep drives over the dock, and the pack is lifted out from underneath. Gravity does the alignment. In simulation that is a real constraint release — the weld opens and the pack becomes a free body. Nothing is teleported.

A Nemotron model is the herd brain: who goes to which patch, who swaps next, and what to do when the dock is blocked or a rover browns out.

Live pitch — the five extra minutes

If you are presenting in person with time for questions, the video script is the talk. Add these only if asked, in this order:

TopicThe one thing to say
Unit economicsPanel, pack, four motors, a frame. The expensive half of a conventional build — mounting, wiring, civils, grid connection — is simply absent. Cost model is live in the sim and updates as you move the sliders.
Why not a tracker?A tracker is a foundation, a motor and a maintenance contract, for one axis. We already have wheels. Aiming is free.
Why trained, not scripted?Scripted driving breaks on real terrain. Scripted insertion is a twenty-year open problem — so we train the approach and solve the last centimetre mechanically, exactly as production battery-swap stations do.
Why is the dock drive-over?Pack underneath, dock below: gravity aligns it. Berth tolerance is ±35 mm and ±9°, which the trained policy comfortably hits at creep speed.
Sim-only?The track’s own rules accept “key application modules in action” with no hardware. The physics is MuJoCo/Isaac, the swap is a real constraint release, and the energy ledger balances to the watt-hour.

Questions to have an answer ready for

“Sheep get stolen. Panels on wheels get stolen faster.”

Every unit is tracked, and a stolen unit is a 30 W, 0.28 m/s machine that has to be carried off a field. More importantly: it is insurable, and a fixed array’s biggest risk — a planning refusal after you have spent the capital — is not.

“Land use is the same. You still cover the field.”

No — the herd is sparse and it moves. The land keeps its use, and the ground is never sealed. That is the difference between renting a field and retiring it.

“Moving parts mean maintenance. Fixed arrays have none.”

True, and it is the real trade. Our answer is that the moving part is deliberately the cheapest thing in the system — four stock gearmotors inside a 30 W envelope — and a broken sheep is driven away and replaced, not repaired in the field on a scaffold.

“Why does it need an LLM? This is a scheduling problem.”

Dispatch is scheduling. Recovery is not. Dock blocked, a rover browning out mid-field, weather turning early — that is open-ended and it is where Nemotron sits. Show the failure clip; do not argue it.

“It’s only simulated.”

Name it before they do — it is in the first minute of the close if you are live. The rules explicitly allow it, and in NVIDIA’s own Cosmos Cookoff first and second place were both simulation-only. Then pivot to the physics fidelity, which is the strong ground.

Recording notes

Record voice-over first and cut picture to it — not the other way round. Read slower than feels right; the cold open in particular wants air between the three sentences.
